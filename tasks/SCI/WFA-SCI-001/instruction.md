# Scoring protein–protein docking decoys (blind prediction)

You are given 10 protein complex targets (C01–C10). Each target has about 1,200–2,600 candidate complex models ("decoys") generated by various docking / structure prediction methods. The vast majority are wrong binding modes; only a few (for some targets only 1–2%) are close to the true structure. The experimental structures of these targets are not available in this task. **Without knowing the true structures**, select from each target's decoy set the 10 models you consider closest to the true binding mode, and rank them.

## Inputs

- `inputs/targets.tsv`: for each target, the number of decoys, the chain identifiers, and the receptor and ligand chains (the interface evaluated is the one between the receptor chain and the ligand chain).
- `inputs/sequences.fasta`: amino-acid sequences of every chain of each target (`>C01_A chain=A length=...`). The two chains of a homodimer have identical sequences.
- `inputs/decoys/<target>/<decoy ID>.pdb`: decoy models, containing only ATOM/TER records. Within a target, all decoys have been superimposed on the receptor chain into the same coordinate frame, and chain identifiers and residue numbering are consistent within the target (the residue ranges of different decoys may differ slightly, and the receptor conformation may also differ); the coordinate frame itself is arbitrary. Decoy IDs look like `C03_D0123`; the numbering order is unrelated to the generating method. The B-factor / occupancy columns carry no information.
- `inputs/decoys.MANIFEST.sha256`: checksum list of the decoy files.

(17,895 decoys in total, about 4.0 GB, materialized into `inputs/decoys/` by the evaluators according to the manifest.)

## Deliverables

Directory `submission/`, one file per target, `submission/<target>.txt`, one model per line, ordered by your confidence from high to low:

```
1 C03_D0123
2 C03_D1877
...
10 C03_D0042
```

- Each line is `<rank> <decoy ID>` with ranks 1–10; only the top 10 are evaluated, extra lines are ignored, and a repeated ID counts only at its first occurrence.
- You may only choose from the decoys given for that target (you may not submit models you modified or built yourself); an ID not in the decoy set is counted as an incorrect model.
- A target that is not submitted is counted as "no correct model".

## Constraints

- Fully offline: do not access the PDB, any structure / sequence database or online service, and do not search for papers, post-challenge results or data related to these complexes; do not use prior knowledge of the true complex structures. You may use general-purpose software installed locally (structure alignment, energy / statistical potentials, interface analysis, clustering, etc.) and code you write yourself.
- Compute resources: a single CPU machine (multi-core parallelism allowed), no GPU.
- You may decompose the task, work in parallel and define your own intermediate artifacts.

## Evaluation

Following the official standard of the scoring experiment of this kind of blind-prediction challenge, the evaluators have precomputed for each decoy its quality class relative to the true structure (high / medium / acceptable / incorrect, based on the fraction of native interface contacts fnat, ligand RMSD and interface RMSD) and its DockQ score. The primary metric is the total number of acceptable-or-better models in your top 10 over the 10 targets (ties are broken by the total number of medium-or-better models). The official measure of that challenge (the number of targets whose best model in the top 10 reaches acceptable / medium / high), the numbers of hits in the top 5 / top 1, and the mean DockQ of the top-1 model are also reported. The result is converted into a rank among the real scorer groups of that challenge round.
