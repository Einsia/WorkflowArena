# Quality assessment of protein complex models (blind prediction)

You are given 12 protein complex targets, each with about 330–375 3D models generated by different structure prediction methods (4343 in total). The experimental structures of these targets are not available in this task. **Without knowing the true structures**, estimate the overall quality and the interface quality of every model and submit them in the required format.

## Inputs

- `inputs/sequences.fasta`: the sequence and stoichiometry of each entity of each target. Header lines look like `>H1236_B entity=B copies=6 stoichiometry=A3B6 length=115` (`A1B1` means one copy each of two different chains, `A3` a homotrimer, `A3B6` three copies of entity A and six of entity B).
- `inputs/models/<target>/<model ID>.pdb`: candidate models (only ATOM/HETATM/TER records; the B-factor column holds the values written by the original prediction method, whose meaning and scale are not guaranteed to be consistent). Model IDs look like `H1215_M0001` and are unrelated to the generating method.
- `inputs/models.MANIFEST.sha256`: checksum list of the model files.

Targets: `H1215 H1223 H1230 H1232 H1236 H1244 H1245` (heteromeric complexes) and `T1201o T1234o T1235o T1259o T1298o` (homo-oligomers).

## Deliverables

Directory `submission/`, one file per target, `submission/<target>.txt`, in the following format (the quality-assessment format of this kind of blind-prediction challenge):

```
PFRMAT QA
TARGET <target>
MODEL 1
QMODE 1
<model ID> <global score> <interface score>
...
END
```

- `global score`: 0–1; higher means the model's overall fold and assembly are closer to the true structure (compared with the TM-score of the whole complex during evaluation).
- `interface score`: 0–1; higher means a more accurate inter-chain interface (compared with interface quality measures during evaluation); write `X` if you do not estimate it.
- Each target must cover at least 80% of its models, otherwise the target is treated as not submitted.

## Constraints

- Fully offline: do not access the PDB, structural databases or any online service; do not search for post-challenge results, papers or data related to these targets. You may use general-purpose software installed locally (structure alignment, energy functions, sequence analysis, etc.) and code you write yourself.
- Compute resources: a single CPU machine (multi-core parallelism allowed), no GPU.
- You may decompose the task, work in parallel and define your own intermediate artifacts.

## Evaluation

For each target the evaluators compute: the Pearson / Spearman correlation between your global scores and the true TM-scores, and the ranking loss (TM-score difference between the truly best model and the model you ranked first); and the correlation and ranking loss of your interface scores against interface quality measures (DockQ, QS-score). These are averaged over the 12 targets; the primary metric is the mean Pearson correlation (global score vs TM-score), which is converted into a rank among the groups of that challenge round in the same category.
